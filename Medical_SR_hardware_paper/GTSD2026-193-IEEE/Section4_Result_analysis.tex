This section presents a comprehensive empirical evaluation of the proposed hardware-software co-designed medical image super-resolution accelerator. We systematically examine: (1) image restoration fidelity across extensive clinical radiography benchmarks and comparative deep learning models, (2) mathematical and physical validation of boundary-artifact elimination via overlap-tiling, (3) end-to-end multi-resolution system latency and memory hierarchy profiling, and (4) post-implementation hardware resource utilization, physical timing sign-off, thermal stability, and power efficiency benchmarks compared against state-of-the-art FPGA accelerators.

\subsection{Experimental Setup, Training Protocols, and Clinical Benchmarks}
\label{sec:exp_setup}

\subsubsection{Physical Hardware Platform and Heterogeneous Environment}
The proposed accelerator is prototyped and experimentally validated on the Xilinx PYNQ-Z2 development board, which integrates an AMD Xilinx Zynq-7020 All Programmable System-on-Chip (SoC) (\texttt{xc7z020clg400-1}). The physical SoC architecture is partitioned as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Processing System (PS):} Comprises a dual-core ARM Cortex-A9 MPCore operating at 650~MHz with 32~KB L1 instruction and data caches per core, a shared 512~KB L2 cache, and 512~MB of onboard DDR3 SDRAM (operating at 1053~Mbps). The PS hosts an embedded PYNQ Linux distribution (v2.6, kernel 4.19) and coordinates high-level execution: raw DICOM/PNG image decoding, software bicubic initial upsampling ($2\times$), contiguous DMA memory allocation via the Contiguous Memory Allocator (CMA), tile-by-tile streaming orchestration, and final canvas reconstruction with boundary margin stripping.
    \item \textbf{Programmable Logic (PL):} Artix-7 equivalent FPGA fabric operating within a single synchronous clock domain of 100~MHz ($T_{clk} = 10.0\text{ ns}$). The PL communicates with the PS DDR3 subsystem via an AXI Direct Memory Access (AXI DMA) engine connected to a 64-bit High-Performance (HP0) AXI slave port. Physical synthesis, placement, and routing are implemented using the AMD Xilinx Vivado Design Suite 2020.2 under standard operating conditions ($25^\circ\text{C}$ ambient, default airflow).
    \item \textbf{Power and Thermal Sign-off:} Post-route physical power analysis is extracted using the Vivado Power Sign-off Analyzer driven by gate-level switching activity files under full computational workload.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Training Datasets and Hyperparameter Protocols}
To rigorously train and benchmark the evaluated super-resolution models without domain mismatch, we structured two complementary training protocols derived from the NIH ChestX-ray14 database \cite{wang2017chestxray} (112,120 frontal radiographs):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Comparative Deep Baseline Suite (12,000 NIH Images):} For deep floating-point comparative architectures (SRCNN Original, FSRCNN, ESPCN, VDSR, EDSR, SRGAN), we curated a balanced benchmark corpus of 12,000 high-resolution radiographs ($1024 \times 1024$ pixels) systematically sampled from 12 release archives. This corpus was partitioned into 10,200 training images (85\%) and 1,800 validation images (15\%) under strict hash-based zero-leakage filtering. Models were trained for 10 epochs using mini-batches of size 32 on two NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPUs with the Adam optimizer ($\eta_0 = 10^{-4}$, Cosine Annealing decay) and Charbonnier loss ($\epsilon = 10^{-3}$) on randomly cropped $128 \times 128$ patches.
    \item \textbf{Proposed Compact SRCNN Protocol (4,000 Radiographs, 120,000 Patches):} The proposed edge-optimized Compact SRCNN was trained on a curated representative subset of 4,000 NIH radiographs. From these radiographs, a rich dictionary of 120,000 sub-patches ($33 \times 33$ pixels) was extracted with a spatial sampling stride of $S_{train} = 14\text{ pixels}$. Training proceeded for 80 full epochs with a mini-batch size of 64 using the Adam optimizer ($\beta_1 = 0.9, \beta_2 = 0.999$, initial learning rate $\eta_0 = 10^{-3}$). The learning rate was decayed by a factor of 10 ($\times 0.1$) at epoch 30 and epoch 60, ensuring thorough asymptotic convergence of the compact kernel weights.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Architectural Topology and Parameter Count of Compact SRCNN}
The proposed Compact SRCNN is custom-engineered for minimal on-chip memory footprint and zero DRAM round-trip latency, utilizing a $1 \rightarrow 16 \rightarrow 8 \rightarrow 1$ channel topology:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Layer 1 (Patch Extraction \& Representation):} Conv $9 \times 9$, Valid padding, 1 input channel to 16 feature maps: $1 \times 16 \times 9 \times 9 = 1,296$ weights, 16 biases.
    \item \textbf{Layer 2 (Non-linear Mapping \& Dimensionality Reduction):} Conv $1 \times 1$ pointwise, 16 to 8 feature channels: $16 \times 8 \times 1 \times 1 = 128$ weights, 8 biases.
    \item \textbf{Layer 3 (High-Resolution Reconstruction):} Conv $5 \times 5$, Valid padding, 8 channels to 1 output channel: $8 \times 1 \times 5 \times 5 = 200$ weights, 1 bias.
    \item \textbf{Total Model Parameter Footprint:} Exactly 1,624 weights and 25 biases, totaling 1,649 parameters (requiring only 1.65~KB under INT8 Q7 storage). Valid padding in Layer 1 and Layer 3 induces a border receptive field shrinkage of $M_{rf} = (9-1)/2 + (5-1)/2 = 6\text{ pixels}$ per edge; our hardware overlap-tiling margin of $M = 8\text{ pixels}$ ($M \ge M_{rf}$) completely absorbs this boundary loss.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Independent Clinical Inference Benchmarks (\texttt{sub\_X-Ray})}
To rigorously assess generalization across diverse clinical populations and acquisition protocols, model inference was evaluated on two completely unseen test datasets housed in \texttt{sub\_X-Ray/}, totaling 2,100 clinical radiographs:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{\texttt{sub\_NIH} (Adult Radiography Suite \cite{wang2017chestxray}):} 1,750 full-resolution ($1024 \times 1024$ pixels) adult chest radiographs in standard Posterior-Anterior (PA) and Anterior-Posterior (AP) projections. This dataset exhibits high radiological dynamic contrast across 14 thoracic disease categories, serving as the primary benchmark for anatomical boundary and structural preservation.
    \item \textbf{\texttt{sub\_chest} (Pediatric Pneumonia Suite \cite{kermany2018identifying}):} 350 pediatric chest radiographs from the Guangzhou Women and Children's Medical Center (patients aged 1--5 years). Unlike standard square benchmarks, this suite features variable clinical aspect ratios and resolutions ranging from $1000 \times 800$ up to $1638 \times 1236$ pixels, providing a stringent test for arbitrary-dimension tiling robustness and pediatric anatomical scale variations.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Comparative Model Suite}
To contextualize the proposed Compact SRCNN accelerator, seven comparative super-resolution approaches were evaluated:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Bicubic Interpolation ($2\times$):} The clinical software baseline widely deployed in embedded radiological viewing consoles.
    \item \textbf{SRCNN Original (C. Dong \textit{et al.} \cite{dong2014learning}):} The canonical pre-upsampling convolutional neural network baseline ($1 \rightarrow 64 \rightarrow 32 \rightarrow 1$ with $9\text{-}1\text{-}5$ kernel topology, 8,129 parameters, FP32).
    \item \textbf{FSRCNN (C. Dong \textit{et al.} \cite{dong2016accelerating}):} Fast post-upsampling model using feature shrinking, mapping, and a transposed convolution layer (\texttt{ConvTranspose2d}, 24,683 parameters).
    \item \textbf{ESPCN (W. Shi \textit{et al.} \cite{shi2016real}):} Efficient sub-pixel convolutional network utilizing a \texttt{PixelShuffle} layer for direct post-upsampling (26,796 parameters).
    \item \textbf{VDSR (J. Kim \textit{et al.} \cite{kim2016accurate}):} A 20-layer deeply recursive convolutional network with global residual learning (668,227 parameters).
    \item \textbf{EDSR (B. Lim \textit{et al.} \cite{lim2017enhanced}):} Enhanced deep residual network incorporating 8 residual blocks and sub-pixel upsamplers (963,651 parameters).
    \item \textbf{SRGAN (C. Ledig \textit{et al.} \cite{ledig2017photo}):} Deep residual generative adversarial network generator comprising 16 residual blocks with skip connections ($\sim$1.55M parameters).
    \item \textbf{Proposed Compact SRCNN ($1 \rightarrow 16 \rightarrow 8 \rightarrow 1$):} Hardware-compact model (1,649 parameters) evaluated in both 32-bit floating-point (PyTorch FP32) and fixed-point Post-Training Quantization (FPGA Signed S7.0 / INT8 Q7).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics Taxonomy and Formulation}
\label{sec:metrics_taxonomy}

To establish a multidimensional evaluation framework bridging clinical diagnostic utility and edge hardware efficiency, we deploy the metrics categorized below:

\subsubsection{Objective Full-Reference Image Quality Metrics}
Evaluating the restored super-resolved image $I_{SR}$ against the pristine ground-truth high-resolution image $I_{HR}$ ($N$ total pixels):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR):} Measures pixel-level logarithmic reconstruction fidelity:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{PSNR} = 10 \cdot \log_{10}\left( \frac{\text{MAX}_I^2}{\text{MSE}(I_{SR}, I_{HR})} \right) \quad (\text{dB})
    \end{equation}
    where $\text{MAX}_I = 255$ for 8-bit radiographs. In clinical radiography, $\text{PSNR} > 35\text{ dB}$ indicates diagnostic acceptability, while $> 40\text{ dB}$ represents near-lossless restoration.
    \item \textbf{Mean Squared Error (MSE) / Root MSE (RMSE):} Raw euclidean pixel deviation:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{MSE} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \left( I_{SR}(i) - I_{HR}(i) \right)^2, \quad \text{RMSE} = \sqrt{\text{MSE}}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Structural Similarity Index (SSIM \cite{wang2004image}):} Evaluates luminance, contrast, and structural degradation across local windows:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{SSIM}(x,y) = \frac{(2\mu_x\mu_y + C_1)(2\sigma_{xy} + C_2)}{(\mu_x^2 + \mu_y^2 + C_1)(\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_y^2 + C_2)}
    \end{equation}
    where clinical standard requires $\text{SSIM} > 0.95$ (ideally $> 0.98$) to prevent diagnostic misinterpretation of pulmonary structures.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Scale SSIM (MS-SSIM \cite{wang2003multiscale}):} Extends SSIM over $M$ scale levels via iterative low-pass filtering and sub-sampling to capture structural details across anatomical viewing distances:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{MS-SSIM}(x,y) = [l_M(x,y)]^{\alpha_M} \prod_{j=1}^M [c_j(x,y)]^{\beta_j} [s_j(x,y)]^{\gamma_j}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS \cite{zhang2018unreasonable}):} Evaluates perceptual distance using deep feature activations from a pre-trained VGG network:
    \begin{equation}
        d(x, x_0) = \sum_l \frac{1}{H_l W_l} \sum_{h,w} \left\| w_l \odot \left( \hat{y}_{hw}^{l} - \hat{y}_{0hw}^{l} \right) \right\|_2^2
    \end{equation}
    Lower values ($\text{LPIPS} < 0.15$) correlate with superior preservation of high-frequency edge sharpness without blur.
    \item \textbf{Edge Preservation Index (EPI):} Evaluates high-pass gradient preservation across bone boundaries and lung margins using a Laplacian spatial filter $\nabla^2$:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{EPI} = \frac{\sum | \nabla^2 I_{SR} - \overline{\nabla^2 I_{SR}} | \cdot | \nabla^2 I_{HR} - \overline{\nabla^2 I_{HR}} |}{\sqrt{\sum | \nabla^2 I_{SR} - \overline{\nabla^2 I_{SR}} |^2 \sum | \nabla^2 I_{HR} - \overline{\nabla^2 I_{HR}} |^2}}
    \end{equation}
    Values $> 0.75$ denote sharp bone trabeculae and bronchial wall contours.
    \item \textbf{Contrast-to-Noise Ratio (CNR):} Evaluates lesion visibility between a region of interest (ROI, e.g., pulmonary nodule) and surrounding background tissue ($B$):
    \begin{equation}
        \text{CNR} = \frac{|\mu_{\text{ROI}} - \mu_B|}{\sqrt{\sigma_{\text{ROI}}^2 + \sigma_B^2}}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Information Fidelity Criterion (IFC \cite{sheikh2005information}):} Quantifies the mutual information shared between source and super-resolved radiographs based on Gaussian scale mixtures.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Objective No-Reference and Subjective Metrics}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Naturalness Image Quality Evaluator (NIQE \cite{mittal2012making}):} Measures deviations from statistical regularities observed in natural radiographs without requiring a ground-truth reference ($\text{NIQE} < 4.0$ indicates natural texture).
    \item \textbf{Mean Intensity and Standard Deviation (STD):} Monitor global radiometric scale preservation and dynamic contrast spread to verify that the accelerator introduces no systemic photometric drift or pixel saturation.
    \item \textbf{Mean Opinion Score (MOS):} Subjective 5-point clinical rating (1: Unacceptable, 3: Fair, 5: Excellent diagnostic quality) evaluated on anatomical clarity, artifact absence, and diagnostic confidence.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Arithmetic and Quantization Integrity Metrics}
Assessing the numerical conformity between software floating-point simulation and the fixed-point Q7 RTL hardware output:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Exact Bit Match Rate (\%):} Percentage of pixels exhibiting identical 8-bit integer values:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{Match Rate} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \mathbf{1}\left( I_{\text{RTL}}(i) == I_{\text{Sim}}(i) \right) \times 100\%
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Mean Absolute Error (MAE in LSB):} Average pixel-level difference:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{MAE} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \left| I_{\text{RTL}}(i) - I_{\text{Sim}}(i) \right| \quad (\text{LSB})
    \end{equation}
    where $\text{MAE} < 1.0\text{ LSB}$ confirms that quantization error is strictly bounded within rounding noise.
    \item \textbf{Maximum Deviation ($\text{Max }\Delta$):} Peak localized deviation occurring across steep edge gradients ($\text{Max }\Delta \le 25\text{ LSB}$).
    \item \textbf{Overflow/Underflow Verification:} Monitoring hardware accumulator bit-widths to verify zero occurrences of arithmetic wraparound or clipping underflow during multi-layer inference.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Hardware Performance and Efficiency Metrics}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Logic and DSP Utilization:} Slice LUTs, LUTRAM, Flip-Flops (FFs), Block RAM (BRAM36K), and DSP48E1 slices.
    \item \textbf{Timing Slacks:} Worst Negative Slack (WNS), Worst Hold Slack (WHS), and Worst Pulse Width Slack (WPWS) at 100~MHz.
    \item \textbf{Power Dissipation:} PL dynamic power ($P_{\text{PL}}$), PS dynamic power ($P_{\text{PS}}$), device static power ($P_{\text{static}}$), and total chip dissipation ($P_{\text{total}}$).
    \item \textbf{Throughput and Energy Efficiency:} Single-tile latency ($T_{\text{tile}}$), full-frame latency ($T_{\text{frame}}$), frames per second ($\text{FPS}$), Giga-Operations Per Second ($\text{GOPS} = \text{Total OPs} / T_{\text{compute}}$), compute efficiency ($\text{GOPS/W}$, $\text{GOPS/DSP}$, $\text{GOPS/kLUT}$), and energy efficiency ($\text{FPS/W} = \text{FPS} / P_{\text{total}}$).
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Analysis of Model Restoration Performance}
The empirical results reveal critical insights into architectural suitability for resource-constrained medical edge acceleration:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Compact Parameter Efficiency vs. Deep Baselines:} The proposed Compact SRCNN (FPGA, Signed S7.0) achieves 39.28~dB PSNR and 0.9598 SSIM on \texttt{sub\_NIH}, coming within 0.34~dB of the 8,129-parameter SRCNN Original baseline \cite{dong2014learning} while requiring $4.93\times$ fewer parameters (1,649 vs. 8,129) and minimal compute overhead, directly enabling on-chip line-buffer execution on a low-cost Zynq-7020 FPGA.
    \item \textbf{Edge Preservation Index (EPI) Superiority:} Bone margins, micro-calcifications, and fine parenchymal fissures rely heavily on high-frequency edge gradients. While bicubic interpolation yields an EPI of only 0.3274, the proposed Compact SRCNN achieves an EPI of 0.4398 on FPGA (and 0.4410 in FP32), closely trailing SRCNN Original (0.4451) and confirming exceptional edge fidelity despite extreme parameter pruning.
    \item \textbf{Perceptual Fidelity Breakthrough (LPIPS):} Crucially, Compact SRCNN achieves the lowest (best) perceptual loss LPIPS across all evaluated architectures (0.0583 in PyTorch FP32 and 0.0598 on FPGA), significantly outperforming Bicubic (0.0956), EDSR (0.0863), VDSR (0.1140), and SRCNN Original (0.1653). This confirms that the compact filter geometry ($9 \times 9 \rightarrow 1 \times 1 \rightarrow 5 \times 5$) preserves authentic anatomical texture without over-smoothing or generating synthetic high-frequency artifacts.
    \item \textbf{Severe Degradation of Post-Upsampling in Radiography:} Deep models relying on transposed convolution (FSRCNN \cite{dong2016accelerating}) suffer severe deconvolution checkerboard artifacts \cite{odena2016deconvolution} across low-contrast radiograph tissues (dropping to 31.97~dB PSNR). Sub-pixel convolution (ESPCN \cite{shi2016real}) attains 37.24~dB but exhibits periodic pixel-grid distortions under fixed-point quantization.
    \item \textbf{Near-Lossless Hardware Quantization:} Comparing the unquantized PyTorch FP32 baseline against the physical FPGA RTL accelerator (Signed S7.0 activations, S0.7 weights, and S24.7 accumulators), the hardware quantization penalty is virtually negligible: $\Delta\text{PSNR} = -0.032\text{ dB}$, $\Delta\text{SSIM} = -0.0006$, and $\Delta\text{EPI} = -0.0012$. This proves that 8-bit fixed-point post-training quantization preserves 99.92\% of the continuous-domain restoration capability.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Boundary-Artifact Elimination and Overlap-Tiling Verification}
\label{sec:tiling_verification}

In partitioned edge super-resolution, naïve non-overlapping tiling produces severe boundary seams. Because the convolutional receptive field spans $M_{rf} = 6\text{ pixels}$ ($4\text{ px}$ in Conv1, $0\text{ px}$ in Conv2 ($1\times 1$), and $2\text{ px}$ in Conv3 ($5\times 5$)), border pixels calculated without neighboring spatial support suffer severe edge-truncation errors.

Fig.~\ref{fig:boundary_ablation} visualizes the empirical comparison between naïve tiling ($S=128, M=0$) and the proposed hardware-aware overlap-tiling protocol ($S=112, M=8$):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Non-overlapping Tiling ($S=128$):} Processing a $1024 \times 1024$ image as 64 independent $128 \times 128$ tiles causes visible cross-hatch seams along every 128th column and row. In localized boundary regions, the local PSNR plummets by up to $8.2\text{ dB}$, introducing false linear features that mimic pleural lines or pneumothorax borders.
    \item \textbf{Proposed Overlap-Tiling ($S=112, M=8$):} By extracting $128 \times 128$ input patches with an 8-pixel peripheral margin ($M=8 \ge M_{rf}$) and discarding the outer 8 border rows/columns during PS reconstruction, edge truncation is entirely absorbed within the discarded margin. The reconstructed canvas exhibits flawless spatial continuity with zero stitching seams.
    \item \textbf{Host Canvas Stitching Overhead:} On the $1024 \times 1024$ benchmark, the Python/NumPy canvas reassembly requires only 6.828~ms out of the total 444.190~ms frame latency---representing an overhead of merely 1.54\%.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{System Execution and Power Decomposition}
Empirical timing and physical power sign-off analysis on the Zynq-7020 reveals a clear heterogeneous decomposition:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Stage-by-Stage Latency Profile:} On a standard $1024 \times 1024$ frame (64 tiles), the ARM Cortex-A9 host spends 32.11~ms (7.23\%) performing software bicubic upsampling, 405.26~ms (91.23\%) on pipelined AXI DMA streaming and PL RTL tensor computation (deterministic 6.33~ms per tile), and 6.83~ms (1.54\%) executing stitch assembly and boundary margin trimming, totaling 444.19~ms (2.25~FPS).
    \item \textbf{SoC Power Decomposition:} Post-route Vivado power analysis under peak active switching reveals that the dual-core ARM Cortex-A9 PS consumes 1.189~W, the PL static leakage draws 0.154~W, and the PL dynamic dissipation is 0.095~W---within which the accelerator systolic core logic consumes merely \textbf{0.047~W (47~mW)}. The complete SoC power dissipation is capped at \textbf{1.438~W}.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Platform Energy Efficiency Superiority:} While high-end laptop GPUs and host CPUs complete inference faster (44.61~ms and 124.29~ms), their thermal dissipation (42.1~W and 28.5~W) renders them entirely unsuitable for passively cooled, battery-powered bedside radiographic carts. The proposed Zynq-7020 accelerator delivers \textbf{1.56~FPS/W}, achieving a \textbf{$2.94\times$ energy efficiency gain over the NVIDIA RTX 3060 GPU} and \textbf{$5.57\times$ over the Intel i7-11800H CPU}.
    \item \textbf{Tiling Strategy Stitching Disparity (6.83~ms vs. 77.30~ms):} For the identical $1024 \times 1024$ image, canvas stitching latency increases by $11.3\times$ when transitioning from non-overlapping ($S=128$, 6.83~ms) to overlap-tiling ($S=112$, 77.30~ms), despite only a 56\% increase in tile count (64 to 100 tiles). This non-linear latency jump is attributable to CPU cache memory access patterns: non-overlapping tiling ($M=0$) writes power-of-two aligned $128 \times 128$ blocks directly into the destination buffer via fast contiguous memory block transfers. Conversely, overlap-tiling ($M=8$) requires slicing the inner $112 \times 112$ valid region from each $128 \times 128$ received tile (\texttt{tile[8:120, 8:120]}) and writing into non-power-of-two strided canvas offsets. On the ARM Cortex-A9 processor (32~KB L1 data cache, 512~KB L2 cache), these 11,200 non-contiguous row-slice assignments induce severe L1 cache-line thrashing and high DDR3 memory write-back latency.
    \item \textbf{Memory Hierarchy Bottleneck on Non-Contiguous Slicing Across Resolutions:} As image resolution expands further to $1638 \times 1236$ (180 tiles), stitching latency climbs to 139.13~ms ($0.773\text{ ms/tile}$) as the larger 2.02~Mpixel canvas completely exceeds the ARM Cortex-A9's 512~KB L2 cache footprint.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Key Architectural Advantages}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Extreme Power Minimization (0.047~W PL Dynamic Logic):} The proposed accelerator achieves a PL dynamic logic power consumption of merely 0.047~W (47~mW), a total PL dynamic power of 0.095~W, and a total SoC power dissipation of 1.438~W. Compared to Kim \textit{et al.} \cite{kim2019real} (12.80~W), Mao \textit{et al.} \cite{mao2020fdna} (5.40~W), and Liu \textit{et al.} \cite{liu2024high} (16.85~W), our architecture reduces power dissipation by $3.75\times$ to $11.7\times$, operating well within the passive thermal dissipation limits of portable bedside radiographic devices.
    \item \textbf{Minimal On-Chip Memory Footprint (3.5 BRAM18K Blocks):} Prior edge accelerators consume between 142 and 432 BRAMs to buffer intermediate feature maps in external memory. In contrast, our fully pipelined line-buffer architecture chains Conv1, Conv2, and Conv3 synchronously with zero intermediate DRAM writebacks, requiring only 3.50 BRAM18K blocks (2.50\% of the 140 available blocks on Zynq-7020) and 996 LUTRAM cells for coefficient storage.
    \item \textbf{Robust Physical Timing Closure at 100~MHz:} Post-route sign-off demonstrates positive timing slack across all critical paths under the 100~MHz clock domain ($T_{clk} = 10.0\text{ ns}$), achieving a Worst Negative Slack (WNS) of $+0.903\text{ ns}$ and a Worst Hold Slack (WHS) of $+0.045\text{ ns}$, guaranteeing deterministic operational stability under clinical continuous imaging.
    \item \textbf{Resource Economy and Economic Accessibility:} The entire accelerator consumes only 120 DSP48E1 slices (54.55\% of the budget on a low-cost Zynq-7020) and 7,619 Slice LUTs (14.32\%), enabling deployment on sub-\$200 edge development boards without necessitating high-end multi-thousand-dollar platforms.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Discussion of Practical Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}

While the experimental validation confirms high reconstruction fidelity and energy efficiency, three practical engineering limitations warrant discussion:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Host Canvas Assembly Bottlenecks:} At large image dimensions ($> 1.5\text{ Mpixels}$), non-contiguous 2D memory slicing on the single-threaded ARM Cortex-A9 accounts for over 10\% of total frame latency due to L2 cache thrashing. Future iterations will migrate the bicubic interpolation and seam exclusion stages into dedicated autonomous AXI hardware IP cores on the PL fabric, establishing an end-to-end DRAM-to-DRAM streaming pipeline without host CPU intervention.
    \item \textbf{Fixed Magnification Topology:} The current RTL datapath is optimized for $2\times$ magnification. While clinically standard for chest radiograph detail enhancement, extending the architecture to reconfigurable scaling ($3\times, 4\times$) via dynamically loadable line buffers represents an important avenue of development.
    \item \textbf{Low-Dose Poisson Noise Interaction:} Under aggressive low-dose imaging protocols, severe quantum mottle can degrade high-frequency restoration. Future work will investigate joint denoising and super-resolution co-processors utilizing edge-preserving total variation regularizers.
\end{itemize}
