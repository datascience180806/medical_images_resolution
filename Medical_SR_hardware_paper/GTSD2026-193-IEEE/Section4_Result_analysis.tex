To validate the clinical efficacy, computational throughput, and hardware efficiency of the proposed co-design framework, we conduct extensive experiments evaluating both image reconstruction fidelity and physical FPGA implementation metrics. This section presents our experimental setup, quantitative and qualitative quality evaluations across 2,100 clinical radiographs, execution profiling, and comprehensive benchmarking against state-of-the-art FPGA super-resolution accelerators.

% =========================================================================
% IV-A. EXPERIMENTAL SETUP AND CLINICAL BENCHMARKS
% =========================================================================
\subsection{Experimental Setup and Evaluation Protocols}
\label{sec:exp_setup}

\subsubsection{Hardware Platform and Deployment Environment}
The end-to-end co-design framework is deployed on the commercial Xilinx PYNQ-Z2 development board hosting a Zynq-7020 SoC (\texttt{xc7z020clg400-1}). The system architecture is partitioned across two distinct domains:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Processing System (PS):} Dual-core ARM Cortex-A9 processor running at 650~MHz with 512~MB DDR3 memory, executing an embedded Linux OS (PYNQ Linux v2.6). The PS coordinates file I/O, initial bicubic pre-interpolation, AXI DMA memory allocation, and tile assembly.
    \item \textbf{Programmable Logic (PL):} Artix-7 equivalent fabric operating within a single 100~MHz clock domain, hosting the proposed streaming RTL accelerator. Data communication between PS and PL is established through a high-performance 64-bit AXI4-Stream Direct Memory Access (DMA) interface.
\end{itemize}
Hardware synthesis, placement, routing, and physical timing/power closure were performed using AMD Xilinx Vivado Design Suite (v2020.2). Power dissipation is measured using the Vivado Power Sign-Off Analyzer under full switching activity vectors captured during physical execution.

\subsubsection{Clinical Radiography Benchmarks}
To ensure robust evaluation without data leakage, all experiments are performed on clinical radiographs strictly held out from model training:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{NIH ChestX-ray14 Benchmark \cite{wang2017chestxray}:} A large-scale clinical dataset comprising 1,750 high-resolution chest radiographs ($1024 \times 1024$ pixels) exhibiting diverse pulmonary conditions (e.g., effusion, pneumonia, nodule, and cardiomegaly).
    \item \textbf{Guangzhou Pediatric Pneumonia Benchmark \cite{kermany2018identifying}:} A high-resolution clinical cohort comprising 350 pediatric chest radiographs with variable resolutions ranging from $1000 \times 800$ up to $1638 \times 1236$ pixels, utilized to verify the arbitrary-resolution robustness of the overlap-tiling protocol.
    \item \textbf{Exploratory Calibration Cohort:} A localized subset of the COVID-19 Radiography database \cite{chowdhury2020can} ($299 \times 299$ pixels) was utilized during initial hyperparameter tuning and fixed-point dynamic range verification before frozen physical evaluation on the 2,100-image clinical benchmark.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Evaluated Baselines and Comparative Configurations}
We benchmark the proposed hardware accelerator against four foundational computing baselines:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Bicubic Interpolation ($2\times$):} The ubiquitous clinical software standard for real-time radiographic upsampling.
    \item \textbf{Floating-Point SRCNN (FP32 Baseline):} The unquantized compact network executed in IEEE-754 single-precision floating-point arithmetic on host CPU/GPU via PyTorch.
    \item \textbf{Bit-Accurate Emulation (Software PTQ Q7):} A software-emulated fixed-point model executing signed integer arithmetic ($\text{S7.0} \times \text{S0.7} \rightarrow \text{S24.7} \gg 7$) identical to RTL logic.
    \item \textbf{Proposed FPGA Accelerator (Hardware PTQ Q7):} The physical pipelined RTL core executing on the Zynq-7020 PL fabric.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Architectural Justification: Pre-Upsampling vs. Post-Upsampling}
As highlighted in Section~II, architectural selection is paramount for edge-class FPGAs. While post-upsampling topologies (such as FSRCNN using transposed convolution or ESPCN using sub-pixel shuffle) compute lower-dimensional feature maps, their hardware implementation imposes critical penalties. Transposed convolution requires periodic zero-insertion across activations, causing 50\%--75\% of MAC operations to multiply by zero, introducing pipeline stalls and notorious checkerboard artifacts \cite{odena2016deconvolution} that can mimic pathological micro-calcifications. Conversely, sub-pixel convolution demands complex multi-channel shuffling logic that disrupts streaming raster access, inflating LUT utilization by over 40\% for dynamic routing multiplexers. By employing pre-upsampling SRCNN with sliding line buffers, our accelerator guarantees an Initiation Interval of $II=1$ with continuous raster streaming, zero idle arithmetic cycles, and complete immunity to checkerboard artifacts.

\subsubsection{Diagnostic Safety and Hallucination-Avoidance Verification}
In medical imaging modalities, preserving diagnostic integrity requires strict guarantees against generative artifacts. Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and diffusion priors are susceptible to \textit{hallucination}---synthesizing visually plausible yet anatomically fictitious textures, such as false nodules or artificial interstitial markings \cite{bhadra2021hallucinations}. 

In our framework, hallucination avoidance is guaranteed deterministically through three architectural constraints:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Deterministic Mapping:} The network topology is purely feedforward, lacking stochastic latent vectors or adversarial feedback loops during inference.
    \item \textbf{Exact Numerical Bounds:} Across all 2,100 clinical radiographs, the Mean Absolute Error (MAE) between hardware fixed-point outputs and software float references is strictly bounded to $\text{MAE} = 0.68\text{ LSB}$ (where $1\text{ LSB} = 1/255$). Maximum localized pixel deviation is strictly below $12\text{ LSB}$, confined exclusively to high-frequency skeletal transitions.
    \item \textbf{Zero Overflow Sign-off:} Hardware execution logs recorded 0 overflow or underflow exceptions, confirming that internal accumulator widening to 24 bits ($S24.7$) completely eliminates numeric clipping distortions.
\end{enumerate}
Qualitative visual inspections confirm that delicate anatomical landmarks---such as pulmonary vessel bifurcations, fine diaphragmatic margins, and trabecular bone striations---are reconstructed cleanly without high-frequency ringing or hallucinatory artifacts.

% IV-C. BOUNDARY-ARTIFACT ELIMINATION AND OVERLAP-TILING ANALYSIS
% =========================================================================
\subsection{Boundary-Artifact Elimination and Overlap-Tiling Verification}
\label{sec:tiling_eval}

To validate Algorithm~1 formulated in Section~III-D, we evaluate the spatial continuity of reconstructed radiographs across patch boundaries. 

When tiling without overlap ($S = 128$, $\text{margin} = 0$), severe boundary seams emerge along the grid lines. These discontinuities stem from the receptive field truncation of the cascaded convolutional layers: Layer~1 ($9 \times 9$) and Layer~3 ($5 \times 5$) induce an edge distortion margin of $M_{rf} = \lfloor 9/2 \rfloor + \lfloor 5/2 \rfloor = 4 + 2 = 6\text{ pixels}$. Along the tile perimeter, pixels computed with truncated receptive fields exhibit localized PSNR drops exceeding $4.8\text{ dB}$, producing visible gridlines across clinical lung fields.

In contrast, our boundary-safe overlap-tiling strategy employs an overlap margin of $M = 8\text{ pixels}$ ($M > M_{rf}$) with stride $S = 112$, paired with peripheral edge-replication padding. During PS-side canvas reassembly, the corrupted 8-pixel exterior halo of each $128 \times 128$ patch is discarded, stitching exclusively the valid interior $112 \times 112$ core into the global image buffer. Across all 2,100 radiographs, this protocol achieves \textbf{100\% seam-free reconstruction}, eliminating edge artifacts entirely while adding negligible software overhead.

\subsubsection{Resolution Scaling and Memory Access Analysis}
When scaling to full-format clinical radiographs of resolution $1638 \times 1236$ ($N_p = \lceil 1638/112 \rceil \times \lceil 1236/112 \rceil = 15 \times 12 = 180\text{ patches}$), total execution time scales to \textbf{1343.39~ms} ($0.74\text{ FPS}$). Pure hardware compute and DMA transfers scale linearly with tile count ($180 \times 6.332\text{ ms} = 1139.76\text{ ms}$, representing 84.84\% of execution). 

Crucially, canvas stitching overhead increases from $6.828\text{ ms}$ ($0.107\text{ ms/patch}$) in the non-overlapping configuration to $139.128\text{ ms}$ ($0.773\text{ ms/patch}$) in the 180-patch overlap configuration. This non-linear scaling is governed by host memory hierarchy effects: in the non-overlapping baseline, full $128 \times 128$ blocks are written directly into contiguous memory; in contrast, overlap stitching requires interior sub-array slicing ($[8:120, 8:120]$) and strided 2D memory copies into a large 2-megapixel NumPy buffer. On the 650~MHz in-order ARM Cortex-A9 core, strided writes induce frequent L1/L2 data cache misses and boundary conditional checks. Nonetheless, hardware compute dominates over 84\% of total latency, confirming balanced system pipelining.

Compared to unaccelerated software execution on the ARM Cortex-A9 host (which requires over 8.2~seconds per megapixel radiograph), our FPGA-accelerated co-design delivers an end-to-end \textbf{speedup of $18.5\times$}.

The comparison highlights four decisive advantages of our proposed co-design architecture:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Minimal Hardware Footprint:} Prior works demand expansive mid-to-high-tier FPGAs (e.g., ZCU102, Kintex-7, or Virtex-7) allocating between 181 and 1,512 DSP slices. In contrast, our microarchitecture consumes only \textbf{120 DSP48E1 slices} and a remarkably low \textbf{3.5 BRAM36K blocks} (2.50\%), fitting comfortably within entry-level edge silicon.
    \item \textbf{Ultra-Low Power Envelope:} Our total SoC power dissipation is \textbf{1.438~W}, with the active PL accelerator core dissipating a mere \textbf{0.047~W} of dynamic logic power. This is $1.4\times$ to $11.7\times$ lower than existing FPGA accelerators (2.08~W--16.85~W), satisfying the sub-1.5~W thermal limit essential for portable, battery-operated bedside imaging.
    \item \textbf{Streaming BRAM Efficiency:} By avoiding intermediate feature writebacks to external DRAM and engineering line-buffer depths precisely to patch pitch, on-chip memory utilization is reduced by over $40\times$ compared to prior works (3.5 BRAMs vs. 142--432 BRAMs).
    \item \textbf{Dedicated Clinical Validation:} While all existing FPGA accelerators exclusively evaluate natural image datasets (Set5, Set14, Urban100), our work provides the first rigorous, large-scale clinical validation on 2,100 chest radiographs, explicitly proving preservation of diagnostic features.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Discussion of Practical Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

While the proposed framework delivers high throughput and energy efficiency, three practical limitations warrant discussion:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Host Software Pre/Post-Processing Overhead:} As demonstrated in Section~\ref{sec:latency_eval}, non-contiguous array slicing during canvas stitching on the ARM Cortex-A9 consumes approximately 10\% of execution time on large images ($1638 \times 1236$). Migrating bicubic interpolation and seam-clipping into dedicated RTL IP cores on the PL fabric represents a promising path toward fully autonomous hardware execution.
    \item \textbf{Fixed Magnification Factor:} The current RTL line-buffer datapath is tailored for $2\times$ super-resolution. Supporting arbitrary or larger scale factors ($3\times, 4\times$) requires reconfigurable line-buffer strides or multi-stage cascade pipelines.
    \item \textbf{Extreme Noise Resilience:} Although effective under standard clinical radiography, low-dose protocols with severe Poisson-Gaussian noise may require integrating an explicit denoising prior into the front-end feature extractor.
\end{itemize}
